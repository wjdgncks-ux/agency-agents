# 한국 소형·1인 PT샵의 불경기(2022–2026) 매출 방어·증대 사례

> Method note (read first): every Korean news site I tried to open directly (news.mt.co.kr, unicornfactory.co.kr, hankyung.com, segye.com, khan.co.kr, project.skhappiness.org, skhappinessimpactreport.oopy.io) failed with DNS errors (`getaddrinfo ENOTFOUND`) through the sandbox proxy. **All findings below come from search-engine snippets of those pages, not from reading the full articles.** Numbers were copied from the snippets as shown. Some details, such as pricing tables and exact dates, could not be checked against the full text. Where a figure is self-reported by a company or appears in a marketing or vendor post, it is labeled.

## Q1. Which new product formats appeared, which shops used them, and what were the results?

### Takeaway
The best-documented Korean cases are not one-trainer shops. They are small studios and small-gym operators that added a **cheaper group or small-group tier** between a low-cost membership and 1:1 PT at 70,000–90,000 KRW per session. Examples are 버핏서울's 200,000–300,000 KRW/month group classes, 어댑핏's 30,000 KRW/session GX with a 240,000 KRW unlimited monthly pass, and 투게더컴퍼니's small-group PT. Others moved into a **senior or rehab niche** (마이베네핏). In each case the reported effect was retention or re-registration and a return to profit, not a higher price per session. I found no well-sourced, named case of a true 1인 PT샵 with audited before/after revenue.

### Cited Findings

**Case 1: 버핏서울 (Butfit Seoul), group training plus turnaround of failing gyms (2020–2026)**
- Pivot: 버핏서울 moved from an online-solution model to running its own gyms. It acquired 3 struggling fitness centers and turned them profitable ("경영에 어려움을 겪던 피트니스센터 3곳을 인수, 흑자전환"). The article is from June 2022. — [머니투데이 2022-06-29 (snippet)](https://news.mt.co.kr/mtview.php?no=2022062919453888733); same article on [유니콘팩토리](https://www.unicornfactory.co.kr/article/2022062919453888733)
- Product: the company targeted the gap between cheap memberships and expensive PT, gathering "고가의 PT 프로그램보다 훨씬 저렴하면서 … 20만~30만원대 다양한 그룹 운동" (varied group classes at 200,000–300,000 KRW) in one place. It also runs a community-based team-training brand, '팀버핏'. — [머니투데이 2022 via search snippet](https://news.mt.co.kr/mtview.php?no=2022062709563843659); [한국경제 "고가 PT 대신 '이것'"](https://www.hankyung.com/article/202206278147i)
- Result reported for the acquired gyms: a monthly loss of 10–20 million KRW during COVID turned into a monthly operating profit of about 30–50 million KRW. This was stated by a company official, so it is self-reported. — [머니투데이 2022 (snippet)](https://news.mt.co.kr/mtview.php?no=2022062919453888733)
- Mechanism: booking and payment software was combined with a community of people who train together. — [한국경제 "운동+커뮤니티로 망해가던 헬스클럽 살렸다…버핏서울 DX 마법" 2022-09](https://www.hankyung.com/article/2022091879781)
- Retention: "일반 헬스장의 재등록률이 낮은 것과 달리 버핏서울은 평균 60% 수준의 재등록률을 유지" (an average re-registration rate of about 60%). Company-reported. — [머니투데이/유니콘팩토리 2026-01](https://www.mt.co.kr/future/2026/01/30/2026012214122143193)
- Crisis context: the CEO (장민우) said the company had only about 200 million KRW in the bank at the end of 2020, enough for two months of operating costs. — [머니투데이 2026-01](https://www.mt.co.kr/future/2026/01/30/2026012214122143193)
- Scale and growth: the company says revenue grew 1,200% from 2021. It has 16 locations in Seoul and raised a 100억 KRW Series B in late 2025, bringing cumulative funding above 200억 KRW. It aims to be Korea's first listed fitness company. — [플래텀](https://platum.kr/archives/275545); [머니투데이 2026-01](https://www.mt.co.kr/future/2026/01/30/2026012214122143193)
- Revenue figures conflict. One analysis says revenue went from 165억 KRW (2024) to 240억 KRW (2025), up 45.45%, with operating loss narrowing from 27억 to 16억 KRW. — [mstacc analysis](https://mstacc.com/blog/columns/business-analysis/gymboxx-vs-buffetground). A financial-data site shows 239억 9,805만 KRW for Dec 2025 but labels it **+29.2% YoY**. — [캐치 재무평가](https://www.catch.co.kr/Comp/CompInfo/JI6015). The 2025 revenue level of about 240억 KRW is consistent between the two; the growth rate is not.
- Strategy analysis framing the case as "exercise for fun" community creation rather than health-motivated PT. — [한국경제 박철순 교수 칼럼 2024-10](https://plus.hankyung.com/apps/newsinside.view?aid=202410168121b&sns=y)

**Case 2: 어댑핏 스튜디오 (Adapfit, PT studio for people with disabilities, SK행복나눔재단 project, 2023–2024)**
- Problem: PT averaged about **75,000 KRW per session** (range 70,000–90,000 KRW), in line with ordinary PT studios but a burden for disabled members who had only received hospital therapy before. Re-registration was treated as the single most important conversion metric. — [SK행복나눔재단 Project Note (snippet)](https://project.skhappiness.org/how-can-a-fitness-studio-for-people-with-disabilities-survive/)
- Six months after opening, re-registrations for the full PT service fell **81% over two months**. — [Project Note (snippet)](https://project.skhappiness.org/how-can-a-fitness-studio-for-people-with-disabilities-survive/)
- New product: a GX (group class) tier at **30,000 KRW per session**, plus a **240,000 KRW monthly unlimited pass**. The pass was priced so members who came twice a week or more would save money. — [Project Note (snippet)](https://project.skhappiness.org/how-can-a-fitness-studio-for-people-with-disabilities-survive/)
- Result: over 3 months, 10 members joined GX. 8 also kept PT and 2 did not. Members who had not re-registered for PT came back through GX, which the organization took as evidence that GX can retain members. The sample is very small. — [Project Note (snippet)](https://project.skhappiness.org/how-can-a-fitness-studio-for-people-with-disabilities-survive/); project phase 2 page: [어댑핏 스튜디오 프로젝트 2단계 (2023.1–2024.12)](https://skhappinessimpactreport.oopy.io/adapfit-02)
- Later demand-side support: SK행복나눔재단 matches wheelchair users with neighborhood gyms and subsidizes up to **300,000 KRW** of fees. This is a third-party subsidy channel for niche studios. — [스페셜타임스](https://www.specialtimes.co.kr/news/articleView.html?idxno=471349)

**Case 3: 투게더컴퍼니, small-group PT brand (2022–2025)**
- Model: small-group training led by professional coaches, positioned as cheaper management than individual PT. — [한국신용신문](https://www.creditnews.kr/news/articleView.html?idxno=31324)
- Result: the first branch opened in 2022 and the brand grew to **5 branches** in about 3 years, with more than **1,500 cumulative members**. — [한국신용신문](https://www.creditnews.kr/news/articleView.html?idxno=31324)
- Prices, session structure and revenue were **not found**.

**Case 4: 마이베네핏, senior and rehab "6070 헬스센터" at 반포역 (2022–2023)**
- Format: an AI-coached, low-staff fitness center inside a subway station. It targets people aged 60+ and cancer survivors, with rehab-exercise data work on sarcopenia, mild cognitive impairment, prediabetes and cancer survivorship. — [한국경제 2022-09](https://www.hankyung.com/article/202209065721i)
- The space started as a test lab. Customer response led the company to make it the first hub center (2023), with expansion planned to other subway-station sites. — [한국경제 2023-08](https://www.hankyung.com/article/202308017068i)
- Revenue: the founder **projected** 500% YoY growth for 2022 (a forecast, not an actual result). — [한국경제 2022-09](https://www.hankyung.com/article/202209065721i). 잡플래닛 lists 2023 revenue as 7억 7천만 KRW. — [잡플래닛](https://www.jobplanet.co.kr/companies/396508/landing/%EB%A7%88%EC%9D%B4%EB%B2%A0%EB%84%A4%ED%95%8F). The founder criticized the industry's prepaid model ("선납입니다. 50만원, 100만원 내고 가지도 않게 되죠"). — [한국경제 2022-09](https://www.hankyung.com/article/202209065721i)
- Price: not found.

**Weaker anonymous or marketing-sourced cases (use with caution)**
- A trainer who left a gym opened a studio of about 20평 (roughly 66 m²). In month 1 there were 8 clients and income was about 60% of the trainer's gym-employee income. After 6 months there were 18 clients and income was about 1.8× the gym income. The causes given were a higher per-session price and no gym commission. This comes from a blog by a PT-management SaaS vendor (piehealthcare), is anonymous, and is unverified. — [포인티 블로그](https://point.piehealthcare.kr/post/%ED%97%AC%EC%8A%A4%EC%9E%A5%EC%9D%84-%EB%82%98%EC%99%80-%EC%B2%AB-pt-%EC%8A%A4%ED%8A%9C%EB%94%94%EC%98%A4%EB%A5%BC-%EC%97%B0-%ED%8A%B8%EB%A0%88%EC%9D%B4%EB%84%88%EA%B0%80-%EB%B0%B0%EC%9A%B4-%EA%B2%83%EB%93%A4)
- An 압구정 PT shop owner said 1:1 PT revenue more than doubled and group classes went from about 2 people per class to fully booked at every time slot. This is a testimonial on a marketing agency's site, so treat it as advertising. — [brandyaction](https://www.brandyaction.com/boards/62351/posts/304051/view)
- A one-person PT shop founded in 2018, with no staff, reportedly averaged 7–10 million KRW of monthly revenue. Consultations fell to one-third during COVID. The source is an aggregator blog and the date is unclear. — [buza.biz 창업뉴스](https://buza.biz/bbs/board.php?bo_table=changup_news&wr_id=4958)
- A management-software guide suggests group "special classes" that deduct 3–5 sessions from an existing pass, such as weekend head-coach classes or family events, as a revenue lever. This is vendor guidance, not a measured result. — [바디코디 가이드](https://crm-guide.bodycodi.com/bodycodi1029)

### Inferences
- The repeated pattern is **tier insertion**. Because 1:1 PT at 70,000–90,000 KRW per session is hard to sell in a squeeze, shops added a group or small-group tier at roughly 25,000–40,000 KRW per session, or about 240,000–300,000 KRW per month. That tier keeps members who would otherwise leave and feeds them back into 1:1 PT (at 어댑핏, 8 of 10 GX members kept PT).
- Community and retention metrics (about 60% re-registration at 버핏서울) are what investors and press cite, more than price per session. This suggests retention was the main growth driver in the downturn.
- Senior and rehab specialization (마이베네핏, 어댑핏) is a recurring niche move, but neither published audited results.

### Gaps
- I found **no named 1-trainer 1인 PT샵 with sourced before/after revenue** in 2023–2026 trade press. The one-person cases above are anonymous or marketing material.
- I did not find documented Korean cases of subscription or monthly PT (월정액 PT), hybrid online and offline PT, or postpartum or office-worker programs with results.
- Full articles could not be opened, so the session structures (class length, group size caps) for 버핏서울 and 투게더컴퍼니 are not confirmed.

## Q2. How did small shops change pricing and retention?

### Takeaway
Market quotes put 1:1 PT at roughly 50,000–120,000 KRW per session. 2:1 or 3:1 PT reportedly cuts the cost per person by 30–50%. There is no official time series on PT price changes. Retention tools that show up in the sources are re-registration tracking and community, plus cheaper group tiers. Some local markets report price cutting.

### Cited Findings
- Typical prices: 10 sessions cost about 700,000–800,000 KRW on average in Seoul and 500,000–600,000 KRW nationwide. The article is about 3 years old. A trainer quoted there says that, except at independently run PT shops, the gym rather than the trainer sets the price, and there is a company-set minimum price per session. About 60% of the fee goes to the center. — [Daum "서울대생 과외보다 비싸다는 PT"](https://v.daum.net/v/Z2T3pWLKfL)
- A 2026 consumer guide gives 1:1 PT at 50,000–120,000 KRW per session and says 2:1 or 3:1 group PT cuts the cost per person by 30–50%. A non-authoritative guide. — [thorkit.kr 2026](https://thorkit.kr/guides/gym-cost-saving-guide/)
- A 2026 guide lists Seoul 1:1 at 60,000–80,000 KRW, 2:1 duet PT at 35,000–50,000 KRW, and 3–5 person small groups at 25,000–40,000 KRW per session. A non-authoritative guide, found via search snippet. — [dasaja.co.kr 2026-03](https://dasaja.co.kr/saja_guide/38)
- Example listing: a gym near 부산대 offers group PT from 25,000 KRW, with registration from 10 sessions (1 month). — [당근 업체 페이지](https://daangn.com/kr/local-profile/%EC%94%A8%ED%88%AC%EC%A7%90-jwnho6n7qy4r/)
- The trade press (피트니스경영신문) notes that PT-centric centers lose revenue immediately when trainers leave or members drop off. It also notes that in some regions low-price competition has pushed price per session **below past levels**. — [피트니스경영신문 (snippet)](https://ifk.co.kr/news/search.php?q=&date_val=1+month&date_val0=2024-12-06&var=page1&page0=7&page2=2&page3=5&page1=1); see also [피트니스경영신문 "회원 수 많다고 성공?… 고객 단가가 관건"](https://ifk.co.kr/news/view.php?no=686), which recommends growing high-value services (PT, Pilates) to raise revenue per customer.
- A trade guide on small PT shops: a 15평 shop is typically run by 1–2 trainers with 0–1 front-desk staff. Revenue is volatile because it depends on the owner's own sessions. Metrics to track are consult-to-sign rate, attendance after the first visit, self-training adherence, and **re-registration rate after PT ends**. — [피트니스경영신문 (snippet)](https://www.ifk.co.kr/news/search.php?search_type=name&q=%ED%94%BC%ED%8A%B8%EB%8B%88%EC%8A%A4%EA%B2%BD%EC%98%81%EC%8B%A0%EB%AC%B8&date_val=1+month&date_val0=2025-11-10&var=page3&page3=6&page0=1&page3=5)
- Revenue recognition: a vendor blog stresses separating pass sales from sessions actually delivered (consumed revenue). — [포인티 "PT센터 매출, 숫자 세 개만 알면 됩니다"](https://point.piehealthcare.kr/post/pt%EC%84%BC%ED%84%B0-%EB%A7%A4%EC%B6%9C-%EC%88%AB%EC%9E%90-%EC%84%B8-%EA%B0%9C%EB%A7%8C-%EC%95%8C%EB%A9%B4-%EB%90%A9%EB%8B%88%EB%8B%A4)
- A gym profitability blog estimates that about 10% of ordinary members convert to PT. This is a blogger's estimate. — [gongysd.com 2026](https://gongysd.com/trainer-lesson-log/?bmode=view&idx=170141240)

### Inferences
- Independent 1인 PT샵 owners set their own prices, while trainers employed by gyms face a company minimum. Owners can therefore build tiered menus (1:1, 2:1, group), and the small studio's income gain comes mainly from **removing the center's cut of about 60%**.
- Downturn pressure seems to show up as split pricing (more 2:1 and small-group options) and local price cutting rather than across-the-board increases. There is no hard index to confirm this.

### Gaps
- There is no official PT price index or 2022–2026 time series. PT is not a separate CPI item in any source I found.
- I found no sourced Korean data on referral programs, membership-pause policies or trends in smaller prepaid packages at small PT shops.

## Q3. What do consumer-protection and industry data say about prepaid PT harm and closures, and how can trustworthy shops use transparency?

### Takeaway
Prepaid-fee damage is large and rising. 한국소비자원 relief requests for gyms, Pilates and yoga went from about 3,200 in 2021 to 4,328 in 2025, and about 92% of cases concern contract cancellation. The FTC's price-display rule covers 체력단련장 (gyms) and, from April 2025, only **children's** 체육교습업. Standalone adult PT shops therefore largely fall outside mandatory price display and have no deposit protection. Voluntary price disclosure and per-session payment are a real way to stand out.

### Cited Findings
- In March 2025 경향신문 reported that, as the recession deepens, PT businesses offering 1:1 or small-group training are increasingly closing for financial reasons without refunding members, and that legal safeguards are lacking. — [경향신문 2025-03-13 (snippet)](https://www.khan.co.kr/article/202503130600031)
- 한국소비자원 relief requests for gyms numbered 10,104 between 2022 and March 2025, and about 92% concerned contract cancellation. — via [뉴시스 2025-11](https://www.newsis.com/view/NISX20251119_0003409197) / search summary. Requests covering gyms, Pilates and yoga were 3,224 in 2021 and 4,328 in 2025 (about +34%). — [세계일보 2026-09-24 (snippet)](https://www.segye.com/newsView/20260924500189). **The 2021 figure appears as both 3,224 and 3,244 in different snippets.**
- 세계일보 estimates **2,910억 KRW** of "헬스장 먹튀" damage. This is an extrapolation assuming only about 10% of victims report, not a measured loss. The article also describes a pattern of "annual fee on installments" offers followed by sudden closure. — [세계일보 2026-09-24](https://www.segye.com/newsView/20260924500189); [베타뉴스 (정준호 의원)](https://www.betanews.net/article/view/beta202609240003)
- Pilates and yoga studios are 자유업 (no permit required). Some register as Pilates, operate as gyms, collect members and then close, which leaves members unprotected. — [세계일보 2026-09 (snippet)](https://www.segye.com/newsView/20260924500189)
- Event-price trap: when members cancel PT bought at a promotional price, some shops recalculate the refund at the regular price. — [오마이뉴스](https://www.ohmynews.com/NWS_Web/View/at_pg.aspx?CNTN_CD=A0003131119) / [뉴시스 2025-11](https://www.newsis.com/view/NISX20251119_0003409197)
- Price-display rule (중요한 표시·광고사항 고시): gyms (체력단련장업) must post service content, price structure and refund rules at the premises and on registration forms. **Online display is not mandatory.** — [경향신문 2026-07-23](https://www.khan.co.kr/article/202607230600081)
- FTC 2024 inspection: 248 of 2,001 gyms were not complying. — [한국피트니스산업협회 / 공정위 2024 점검 결과](https://www.kfcf.or.kr/news/news/read.do?no=3043); [일간NTN](https://www.intn.co.kr/news/articleView.html?idxno=2041878)
- April 2025 amendment: **체육교습업** was added to the price-display duty. It is defined as sports instruction for **children under 13** lasting 30 days or more (swimming, baseball, soccer and similar), with a 6-month grace period. — [아주경제 2025-04-22](https://www.ajunews.com/view/20250422095126881); [머니투데이 2025-04-22](https://www.mt.co.kr/economy/2025/04/22/2025042208354348815); [공정위 보도자료](https://www.ftc.go.kr/www/selectBbsNttView.do?pageUnit=10&pageIndex=1&searchCnd=all&key=12&bordCd=3&searchCtgry=01%2C02&nttSn=46018)
- FTC 2025 inspection (published Feb 2026): 2,000 gyms and 300 체육교습업 sites were checked. Overall compliance was 92.5% (7.5% non-compliant), and 26.7% of 체육교습업 sites were non-compliant. Fines can reach 1억 KRW. — [뉴스핌 2026-02-27](https://www.newspim.com/news/view/20260227001005); [아시아경제 2026-02-27](https://view.asiae.co.kr/article/2026022710465806933)
- Even with about 95% reported compliance, PT prices online are often shown only as "변동" (variable), or are given only after an in-person consultation ("상담 후 알려드려요"). — [경향신문 2026-07](https://www.khan.co.kr/article/202607230600081); [농민신문 2025-02-24](https://www.nongmin.com/article/20250224500466)
- A protective bill on gym closures was reported as stalled in the National Assembly. — [더스쿠프](https://www.thescoop.co.kr/news/articleView.html?idxno=310723)
- Fraud example: a PT shop owner raised 17억 KRW from investors by promising "연 20% 수익" (20% annual returns) and misused the funds. — [로톡뉴스](https://lawtalknews.co.kr/article/MDTN3G4RA52C)

### Inferences
- Adult PT studios are not covered by the gym price-display rule. The children-only 체육교습업 extension does not include them, so their prices are not publicly visible. A small shop that **publishes its full price list online, sells per session or monthly instead of large prepaid bundles, and states a clear refund rule** is directly addressing the most common complaint, contract cancellation (about 92% of cases). This is an inference; I found no Korean case that measured the revenue effect of doing so.
- 마이베네핏's founder framed anti-prepayment as a selling point (see Q1), which is the one sourced example of using this positioning.

### Gaps
- I found no source on PT-specific deposit or guarantee insurance (보증보험) products adopted by small shops, or on their uptake.
- 한국소비자원 original press releases were not reached. The figures come through news reports.

## Q4. What macro data exist on gym closures, openings and consumer spending, 2023–2026?

### Takeaway
Closures of registered 체력단련장 (gyms), counted in 행정안전부 licensing data, hit record highs in 2024–2025, at roughly 550–577 a year depending on the source. The 2026 count reached 531 by mid-September. Card data show fitness spending growing through 2023, and I found no direct card-data evidence of a 2024–2025 drop in gym spending.

### Cited Findings
- 체력단련장 closures (행정안전부 지방행정 인허가 데이터):
  - 2020: 432; 2021: 404; 2022: 323. — [세계일보 2026-09 / search summary](https://www.segye.com/newsView/20260924500189)
  - 2024: reported as 570 in some articles, and as 553 (+26.8% YoY) in another. — [네이트/세태 기사](https://news.nate.com/view/20250315n00337); [더스쿠프](https://www.thescoop.co.kr/news/articleView.html?idxno=310723)
  - 2025: reported as 562, 553 or **577**. The last figure comes from 정준호 의원실 and is called the most since records began in 1990. — [네이트 2026-02-01 "위고비·러닝에 밀린 헬스장…작년만 562곳 폐업"](https://m.news.nate.com/view/20260201n11054); [베타뉴스 2026-09-24](https://www.betanews.net/article/view/beta202609240003)
  - 2026: **531 closures by mid-September**, which puts 2026 on track for a new record. — [세계일보 2026-09-24](https://www.segye.com/newsView/20260924500189)

### Inferences
- The closure wave falls on general gyms, while spending moved toward Pilates, yoga, tennis and running. That suggests the squeeze is a **category shift plus price sensitivity**, not a collapse in exercise spending. Small PT shops that offer group, community or niche formats are positioned on the growing side of that shift.
- Licensing data cover only registered 체력단련장. Many PT studios and Pilates shops are 자유업 and do not appear in these closure counts, so true small-PT closures are likely undercounted.
