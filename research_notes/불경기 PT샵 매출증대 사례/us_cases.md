# US Personal-Training Studios & Small Boutique Fitness Businesses: How They Grew or Protected Revenue in Downturns (2008–2010, 2020–2021, 2022–2026)

> Method note for the report writer: The research environment blocked direct page fetches (egress proxy refused twobrainbusiness.com, ericcressey.com, asbn.com, ideafit.com, americanspa.com, franchisechatter.com, fddexchange.com, usekilo.com). All findings below come from search-engine extracts of the cited pages, not full-page reads. Numbers are reported as the search extracts gave them; treat exact wording as "per source page" and re-verify before publication where flagged. Much of the richest case-level data comes from **Two-Brain Business** and **Kilo / Gym World**, which sell consulting or software to gym owners (a commercial interest; self-selected samples). Franchise figures come from **third-party FDD aggregators**, not from the FDD PDFs.

## Q0. Documented US cases (objective: at least 4 concrete, sourced cases)

### Takeaway
Seven US cases are documented with numbers. The strongest are Aspire Fitness (NJ), Iron Tribe Fitness (AL-based franchise), BOLT Fitness (NJ), Alloy Personal Training (GA-based franchise) and a rural Alabama gym that raised prices. Cressey Sports Performance (MA) and Fitness Together add pricing and structure data. All successful cases share one pattern: they moved from 1:1 session packs or cheap group classes to **semi-private / small-group PT (4–6 clients per coach) sold as a monthly recurring membership at $300–$600/month**. That raised average revenue per member (ARM) and revenue per coach-hour while keeping clients for 3.5–4.5+ years.

### Cited Findings

**Case 1: Aspire Fitness (Monmouth County, New Jersey; owner Brian Bott and partners). Semi-private model.**
- Semi-private training is "over 80, 85% of our business" (Bott, per Two-Brain) — [Two-Brain: Secrets of Semi-Private Training and Monster ARM](https://twobrainbusiness.com/bott-arm-leader/)
- ARM was $426/month in one reported month. Two-Brain flags this as a single-month data point, and a later article says it may be misremembering the figure — [Two-Brain: bott-arm-leader](https://twobrainbusiness.com/bott-arm-leader/); [Two-Brain: How This Gym Owner Hires Coaches Who Stay for a Decade](https://twobrainbusiness.com/hire-right-coaches/)
- Retention: clients stay "three and a half years on average," which Two-Brain calls the most consistent metric — [Two-Brain: bott-arm-leader](https://twobrainbusiness.com/bott-arm-leader/)
- Scale: three semi-private gyms doing "over $1.5 million a year in revenue". As of November 2023 the business was "on pace to hit $1.3 million" — [Two-Brain: hire-right-coaches](https://twobrainbusiness.com/hire-right-coaches/); [Two-Brain: Brian Bott profile](https://twobrainbusiness.com/about/brian-bott/)
- Staff policy: the head coach at Colts Neck has stayed 10 of the gym's 11 years, and only two people were fired in 11 years. Aspire **used to pay coaches per client**, which made shift coverage "a math problem" and hurt clients, because coaches only offered make-up times that fit their own schedules. The source extract shows the switch away from per-client pay but not the new pay structure — [Two-Brain: hire-right-coaches](https://twobrainbusiness.com/hire-right-coaches/)
- Owner rationale: semi-private helps keep the best employees and "makes sales easier because you have more offerings" — [Two-Brain: hire-right-coaches](https://twobrainbusiness.com/hire-right-coaches/)

**Case 2: Iron Tribe Fitness (US franchise based in Birmingham, AL). Added semi-private to a group-class model.**
- After adding semi-private training, ARM rose "from the low $200s to $383". Total revenue "barely moved" but profit margin **doubled**. Members stay an average of **56 months** across the owner's eight gyms. This is a Two-Brain account of one multi-unit owner (named "Jamie" in the extract, possibly Jamie Warren; verify), not a brand-wide figure — [Two-Brain: Adding Semi-Private Training to a Fully Booked Gym Schedule](https://twobrainbusiness.com/add-semi-private/)
- Brand context: the 2021 FDD Item 19 reports average gross sales of $433,583.62 across 24 franchised facilities (third-party summary) — [Franchise Chatter: Iron Tribe FDD](https://www.franchisechatter.com/2021/12/30/fdd-talk-how-much-does-an-iron-tribe-fitness-franchise-make-average-revenues-and-or-profits/)

**Case 3: BOLT Fitness & Performance Training (Chester, NJ; owner Mike Baker; opened 2016). Pandemic-era change.**
- Reported about $900k/year in revenue. Mix: 50% semi-private, 30% sports performance, 10% small group, 10% 1:1 — [Kilo: Gym owner makes $900k/yr from semi-private training](https://usekilo.com/gym-owner-makes-900k-yr-from-semi-private-training/); [Gym World (John is building)](https://blog.johnisbuilding.com/p/gym-owner-makes-900kyr-semiprivate-training)
- The original semi-private program capped sessions at 6 clients. **After COVID**, Baker added a small-group program (up to 12 per class, generalized fitness, higher price point). Average client spend rose **from $236/month to $350/month**. The write-ups are about two years old (c. 2024) — [Kilo](https://usekilo.com/gym-owner-makes-900k-yr-from-semi-private-training/); [Gym World](https://blog.johnisbuilding.com/p/gym-owner-makes-900kyr-semiprivate-training)

**Case 4: Alloy Personal Training (founded Atlanta, 1992, by Rick Mayo; franchising since 2020). Small-group PT on monthly membership.**
- Model: Mayo says that "by the 2000s" Alloy had "learned how to scale personal training" through small-group training. Target clients are adults aged roughly 40/45–60/65 — [ASBN interview with Rick Mayo](https://www.asbn.com/interviews/how-rick-mayo-turned-his-personal-training-brand-into-a-nationwide-franchise/); [Alloy blog: How Alloy Became a Scalable Franchise](https://alloyfranchise.com/blog/how-alloy-personal-training-became-a-franchise/)
- Group size up to 6 per coach — [Alloy Falls Church studio page](https://fallschurch.alloypersonaltraining.com/)
- Monthly membership pricing at sample studios: Pinnacle Hills (AR) Standard $379 / Elite $499; Cedar Hills Crossing (OR) Standard $479 / Elite $599 — [Alloy Pinnacle Hills memberships](https://pinnaclehills.alloypersonaltraining.com/memberships); [Alloy Cedar Hills Crossing pricing](https://cedarhillscrossing.alloypersonaltraining.com/membership-pricing)
- Item 19 (2024 FDD) covers 2023 monthly revenue, membership counts, ARM and retention for 1 affiliate-owned and 9 franchised facilities. A third-party summary lists **average revenue/member/month $347** and **average retention 90%**. This is secondary and the retention definition (monthly?) is unverified — [FDD Exchange: Alloy 2024 FDD](https://fddexchange.com/fdd/alloy-personal-training-2024/); [Franzy: Alloy](https://franzy.com/franchises/alloy-personal-training)
- Average unit revenue figures conflict across aggregators: $400,993 (2023), $386,914 (2025 FDD/2025 data), $554,978 (2026 FDD). One listing reports a -4% YoY change from 2024 to 2025 (average $396K) — [Franzy](https://franzy.com/franchises/alloy-personal-training); [BizBuySell](https://www.bizbuysell.com/franchise-for-sale/alloy-personal-training/?f_bfslp=1); [LoopNet](https://loopnet.ca/biz/franchise-for-sale/alloy-personal-training); [Franchise Chatter 2025 review](https://www.franchisechatter.com/2025/05/07/alloy-personal-training-franchise-review-2025-costs-fees-news-average-revenues-and-or-profits/)
- Growth through the inflation period: 3 outlets at end of 2020, 13 at end of 2022, then +46 locations in 2024 and +61 in 2025, reaching 135+ in 38 states. Mayo said COVID delayed franchising until roughly mid-2021 — [Franchise Chatter 2025](https://www.franchisechatter.com/2025/05/07/alloy-personal-training-franchise-review-2025-costs-fees-news-average-revenues-and-or-profits/); [Franchise Times: CapitalSpring investment](https://www.franchisetimes.com/franchise_mergers_and_acquisitions/personal-training-franchise-to-accelerate-growth-with-investment-from-capitalspring/article_ced0775c-b00e-449b-8b70-afaecb79fed9.html)
- Royalty 7% of gross sales. The brand/ad fund fee is reported as 2% by some aggregators and as an 8% "marketing fee" by another (conflict) — [Franzy](https://franzy.com/franchises/alloy-personal-training); [Franchise Overview](https://franchiseoverview.com/company/alloy-personal-training)

**Case 5: Rural Alabama gym (owner Tres Kennedy). Stepwise price increases during the inflation era.**
- The new-client price rose **$150 → $194 → $250 (held several years) → $285/month** "without a meaningful drop in signups." The town has about 1,000 people and the draw area fewer than 10,000. The old $150 plan was 3 PT sessions plus unlimited group classes. The current $285 offer is a **3-month commitment** with 1 PT session per week plus 2 group classes per week. Article by John Franklin. The extract dated it October 7, 2026; verify the date — [Two-Brain: How a Small-Town Gym Nearly Doubled Its Price Without Losing Sales](https://twobrainbusiness.com/gym-doubles-price/)

**Case 6: Cressey Sports Performance (Hudson, MA; founded 2007, at the start of the Great Recession). Semi-private as the default format.**
- Semi-private is the most common format, with a maximum 5:1 client-to-coach ratio. Monthly tiers: **$399** for programming plus unlimited training (up to 6 days/week), **$369** for 8 sessions/month, **$219** for 4 sessions/month. The pricing page is about 8 years old, and current prices are by request only — [Boston Voyager interview: Eric Cressey & Pete Dupuis](https://bostonvoyager.com/interview/meet-eric-cressey-pete-dupuis-cressey-sports-performance-hudson-ma/); [CSP Personal Training (MA)](https://cresseyperformance.com/personal-training-ma/)
- Other products: Strength Camps (up to 15 participants, 60 minutes) and a 10-week development program ($3,499, or $146/month) — [CSP Development Program](https://cresseyperformance.com/developmentprogram/)
- No revenue or retention figures were found for CSP.

**Case 7: Unnamed Two-Brain client gym (location not stated; may not be US). Group classes replaced by semi-private, February 2024.**
- ARM rose **$269 → $464**, and monthly revenue rose **38% with 20% fewer clients** — [Two-Brain: Recession-Proof Revenue](https://twobrainbusiness.com/recession-proof-revenue/)

**Contrast case: Fitness Together (1:1-centric studio franchise).**
- Item 19 (2024 FDD summary): average revenue $460,013, median $401,377, **same-studio revenue +5.1% YoY**. But the unit count fell from **126 franchised outlets (start of 2021) to 96 (end of 2023)**. Another source shows 96 → 93 in 2024 (conflict on 2024) — [FDD Exchange: Fitness Together 2024](https://fddexchange.com/fdd/fitness-together-2024/); [Franzy: Fitness Together](https://franzy.com/franchises/fitness-together)
- Other aggregators: $484,193 average for mature studios with the top unit at about $1.79M (BizBuySell), and $534,267 in 2025. Athletech's tiered figures ($1.2M top, $844K mid, $134K low) are unsourced — [BizBuySell: Fitness Together](https://m.bizbuysell.com/franchise-for-sale/fitness-together/); [Athletech News](https://athletechnews.com/franchise/open-a-fitness-together-franchise-what-you-need-to-know/)

### Inferences
- The repeatable mechanism has two levers. **Revenue per coach-hour** goes up (4–6 clients each paying $35–$70 per session beats one client paying $70–$100). **Price per client** goes down compared with 1:1, which widens the buyer pool in downturns. The monthly recurring format smooths cash flow.
- Iron Tribe and Case 7 show that semi-private can raise profit even when headcount falls. Fewer, higher-value clients mean lower acquisition need and better retention, which is a defensive posture suited to recessions.
- Fitness Together's falling unit count alongside rising same-studio sales suggests survivor bias. Surviving 1:1 studios did fine, but the weaker ones closed in 2021–2023. This is an inference, not a documented cause.

### Gaps
- No primary FDD PDFs were read. Alloy's Item 19 member counts, ARM, retention definition and revenue mix are unverified.
- Aspire's current coach pay model and BOLT's exact semi-private price sheet were not found.
- Results Fitness (Newhall, CA; founded 2000 by Alwyn and Rachel Cosgrove; named a top-10 US gym by Men's Health) is known for semi-private training, but **no revenue, retention or recession-period data** was found — [SCV Elite Magazine profile](https://scvelitemagazine.com/life-love-innovative-results-alwyn-rachel-cosgrove/page/8/)
- "The Training Station" could not be identified or verified.

## Q1. Who switched from 1:1 session packs to semi-private / small-group PT, and what happened to revenue per trainer hour and retention?

### Takeaway
Verified switchers with outcome data are Iron Tribe (ARM low $200s → $383, margin doubled, 56-month tenure), Aspire (80–85% semi-private, 3.5-year tenure) and the Case 7 gym (+38% revenue). **Mike Boyle Strength & Conditioning was NOT verified as having dropped 1:1.** It still offers 1:1 alongside semi-private (2–4 clients) and adult group training. Revenue-per-hour evidence comes from consultancy worked examples, not audited studio data.

### Cited Findings
- MBSC (Woburn, MA; co-founded 1996) currently offers adult group training, 1:1 personal training and semi-private (typically 2–4 people per trainer, positioned as "more cost-effective than traditional 1 on 1"). Boyle says that in the last decade MBSC "moved heavily into personal training and adult fitness." No source says MBSC stopped 1:1 — [Alignable: MBSC personal training](https://www.alignable.com/woburn-ma/mike-boyle-strength-conditioning/personal-training); [Boston Voyager: Michael Boyle](https://bostonvoyager.com/interview/meet-michael-boyle-mike-boyle-strength-conditioning-woburn-middleton/)
- Revenue-per-hour comparison: one owner (Woodslawn Fitness, Daniel Purington; location unverified) takes in **$230/hour serving 6 semi-private clients vs. about $80/hour serving 6 in a big-group class** — [Two-Brain search extract; Small Gyms / semi-private articles](https://twobrainbusiness.com/small-gym-model/)
- Coach pay: Chris Cooper (Two-Brain) says a coach could go from about **$32/hour to $80/hour** under semi-private — [Two-Brain: Semi-Private Training: How Coaches Can Earn $80 or More Per Hour](https://twobrainbusiness.com/coaching-80-dollars-per-hour/)
- Pricing rule of thumb from consultancies: semi-private rate = (1:1 rate × 2) ÷ 3, so a $100 1:1 session becomes about $66.66 per semi-private client. One example puts four clients at $35 each = $140/hour vs. $70 for 1:1. Coaches start at about 33% of session revenue, rising to a maximum of about 44%. When converting 1:1 clients, give **about a 10% discount and no more**. These figures come from search extracts across Two-Brain and Gymkee pages; per-figure attribution is unverified — [Two-Brain: Semi-Private vs Small-Group](https://twobrainbusiness.com/semi-private-vs-small-group-training/); [Gymkee: semi-private pricing](https://gymkee.com/blog/semi-private-personal-training-pricing/)
- Format definition: semi-private means 4–6 people on individualized programs with one coach. Two-Brain claims SGPT clients pay "nearly $100 more per month" than big-group members, and that retention with 4–6 people beats groups of 13. The article itself says "I'm not trying to prove that SGPT or semi-private is better" — [Two-Brain: Miracle Model? What the Data Says About Small Group PT](https://twobrainbusiness.com/small-group-pt-data/)
- Two-Brain leaderboard: in July 2023 every Top-10 ARM gym was above $427, with a Top-10 average of $533 (Dec 2023: $537). Only the top 5 were above $400 at the start of 2023. Two-Brain attributes the rise to semi-private adoption — [Two-Brain: Recession-Proof Revenue](https://twobrainbusiness.com/recession-proof-revenue/)
- Historical baseline: IDEA's 2007 survey found 1:1 offered by 80% of respondents, 2-client sessions by 71% and 3–5-client groups by 44%. Monkey Bar Gymnasium (Madison, WI) reported about 60% of sessions still 1:1 with "small-group training growing rapidly" (article date unverified, roughly 2008–2010) — [IDEA: A New Business Model: Partner & Small-Group Training](https://www.ideafit.com/new-business-model-partner-small-group-training/)

### Inferences
- Revenue per coach-hour typically rises 2–3x when 1:1 is replaced by 4–6-person semi-private at a lower per-client price. Coach take-home can also rise, which supports staff retention (Aspire's decade-long coaches).
- Retention improves mostly because of monthly recurring billing and the community effect, but no controlled study isolates this.

### Gaps
- No independent (non-vendor) dataset on revenue per trainer hour or churn before and after a switch.
- Results Fitness and MBSC switch outcomes are undocumented. Do not claim they "switched" with numbers.

## Q2. Who moved from prepaid packages to recurring monthly memberships (EFT), and what did it do to cash flow and churn?

### Takeaway
Almost every documented growth case (Alloy, Aspire, Iron Tribe, CSP, BOLT, Tres Kennedy) sells **monthly memberships, sometimes with a minimum term**, rather than session packs. Tenure metrics are strong: Aspire 3.5 years, Iron Tribe 56 months, and Alloy a reported 90% retention. No source documented a before/after cash-flow comparison for a specific studio converting from packs to EFT.

### Cited Findings
- Alloy sells tiered monthly memberships (Standard/Elite, $379–$599) and discloses ARM ($347) and retention (90%) in Item 19 (secondary summary) — [Alloy Pinnacle Hills](https://pinnaclehills.alloypersonaltraining.com/memberships); [FDD Exchange: Alloy 2024](https://fddexchange.com/fdd/alloy-personal-training-2024/)
- CSP sells monthly tiers ($219 / $369 / $399) instead of packs (dated) — [Boston Voyager: Cressey](https://bostonvoyager.com/interview/meet-eric-cressey-pete-dupuis-cressey-sports-performance-hudson-ma/)
- Tres Kennedy's current offer is a 3-month commitment at $285/month — [Two-Brain: gym-doubles-price](https://twobrainbusiness.com/gym-doubles-price/)
- The Iron Tribe case reports 56-month average tenure, and Aspire 3.5 years — [Two-Brain: add-semi-private](https://twobrainbusiness.com/add-semi-private/); [Two-Brain: bott-arm-leader](https://twobrainbusiness.com/bott-arm-leader/)
- Industry contrast (2008 recession): an informal poll of Club Industry 2008 seminar attendees found 75% selling fewer new memberships, **60% with higher monthly attrition** and 75% selling less personal training and ancillaries — [American Spa / Club Industry: Membership sales and attrition are upside down](https://www.americanspa.com/commercial-clubs/membership-sales-and-attrition-are-upside-down)

### Inferences
- Recurring billing turns the studio's revenue into a predictable base. In a downturn this avoids the "pack runs out, client doesn't renew" cliff typical of prepaid packs. The evidence is indirect, from tenure numbers, not a measured pack-to-EFT comparison.

### Gaps
- No studio-level before/after data on cash flow or churn from a pack-to-EFT conversion was found. Two-Brain's retention guides (e.g., [Retention Guide 2022 on Scribd](https://www.scribd.com/document/599450418/Two-Brain-Business-Retention-Guide-2022-Update)) may contain this but could not be read.

- The industry shifted from free trials and discount challenges to **assessment-led consultations that sell a prescribed, higher-priced program**. The claimed benefit is higher ARM and better-fit clients, not raw volume.

## Q4. 2008–2010: what did owner-operated studios do to survive and grow?

### Takeaway
Contemporary data shows PT demand fell and consumers moved to budget and group formats. Trainers responded with **group / partner / boot-camp formats and some price cuts**. "Group personal training" entered ACSM's top-10 trends for 2010 explicitly because of the economy. However, **no named US studio's 2008–2010 revenue result was found**. The semi-private studio model (CSP founded 2007; Results Fitness; Alloy's small-group model "by the 2000s") existed then, but recession-specific performance is undocumented.

### Cited Findings
- ACE 2009 survey: 52% predicted fewer people would hire personal trainers and 48% predicted gym memberships would decrease. Budget-friendly and boot-camp workouts topped ACE's 2009 trend list — [Today's Dietitian: 2009's Top 10 Fitness Trends](https://todaysdietitian.com/newarchives/040609p42.shtml)
- "Given the economic climate," group personal training moved into the #10 spot in ACSM's 2010 survey after sitting around #20 — [IDEA: How much do you charge for your PT services?](https://www.ideafit.com/how-much-do-you-charge-for-your-personal-training-services-how-did-you-arrive-at-this-price-point/) (search extract; trend attribution per source)
- A Phoenix-area independent trainer lowered fees because of the downturn, preferring a lower price with longer retention over constantly finding new clients — [IDEA: How much do you charge…](https://www.ideafit.com/how-much-do-you-charge-for-your-personal-training-services-how-did-you-arrive-at-this-price-point/)
- A Dec 2009 trainer blog advises cutting per-client cost while raising total revenue by training more people in groups — [Body Acumen: Make Your PT Business Recession-Proof (2009)](https://bodyacumenllc.com/2009/12/make-your-personal-training-business-recession-proof/)
- Club Industry 2008 informal poll: 75% selling less PT and ancillaries, and low-price clubs gaining share from members who left higher-priced clubs. Industry consultant Scudder: "Our industry got clobbered in the final year of that recession." About 54% of operators expected 2010 revenue to rise over 2009 — [American Spa / Club Industry: membership sales and attrition](https://www.americanspa.com/commercial-clubs/membership-sales-and-attrition-are-upside-down); [American Spa: 2010 holds challenges](https://www.americanspa.com/commercial-clubs/2010-holds-challenges-for-health-club-industry)
- Hindsight from Two-Brain's founder: if they had known about semi-private in 2008, they would have switched from 1:1 to semi-private and kept costs low — [Two-Brain via search extract: small-group-pt-data / Chris Cooper blog](https://www.goodreads.com/author/show/18311803.Chris_Cooper/blog?page=9)
- Cressey Sports Performance opened in 2007 with semi-private as its core format and still operates (MA plus FL) — [Boston Voyager: Cressey](https://bostonvoyager.com/interview/meet-eric-cressey-pete-dupuis-cressey-sports-performance-hudson-ma/)

### Inferences
- The 2008–2010 lesson is directional. Clients traded down from 1:1, and operators who offered a **mid-priced group/semi-private tier** kept them instead of losing them to low-price clubs. Firm revenue evidence for specific studios is missing.

### Gaps
- No named US owner-operated studio with 2008–2010 revenue numbers was found. Candidate sources (Club Industry 2009 archives, Results Fitness and Fitness Together interviews from 2009–2010) were not reachable.
- The "15 lessons the recession taught club owners" article ([American Spa](https://www.americanspa.com/commercial-clubs/15-lessons-recession-taught-club-owners)) could not be read.

## Q5. 2020–2021 pandemic and 2022–2026 inflation: price increases vs. retention, value-based offers, hybrid coaching

### Takeaway
Pandemic: studios pivoted to virtual, outdoor and equipment rental. Results were mixed: one Washington studio lost 20%, one Connecticut studio had "one of the best years ever," and BOLT added a higher-priced small-group tier that lifted spend per client from $236 to $350. Inflation era: per Two-Brain's dataset, **median revenue rose in 2024–2025 for 1:1 studios ($13K → $16K/month) and especially small-group PT gyms ($11K → $16,667/month)**. A rural Alabama gym nearly doubled price with no meaningful drop in signups. Average US gym prices rose only about 14% (2019–Aug 2022), below inflation, suggesting many owners under-priced.

### Cited Findings
**Pandemic (2020–2021)**
- Northwest Personal Training (Clark County, WA) lost about 20% of revenue compared with 2019. The owner renovated, updated the website, spaced out sessions, and kept online workouts alongside in-home sessions — [Vancouver Business Journal (search extract)](https://www.vbjusa.com/?p=31292)
- Brian Buturla (personal training and yoga studio, Norwalk, CT): "I have had one of the best years ever" — [Westfair Online (search extract)](https://westfaironline.com/?p=132372)
- Noel Davis (Paris Fit Studios, Philadelphia) started internet training and gave away complimentary online classes to bring people in. Clients initially resisted and lacked equipment — [Philadelphia Inquirer, Jan 20, 2021](https://www.inquirer.com/health/wellness/pandemic-fitness-noel-davis-philadelphia-20210120.html)
- Matt Hoodie (Transform Scottsdale, AZ) launched a **12-week nutrition education platform** when the studio closed — [Pocket Prep Q&A](https://www.pocketprep.com/posts/qa-with-gym-owner-whos-business-thrived-during-covid)
- BOLT Fitness (Chester, NJ) added a higher-priced small-group tier after COVID, and average client spend rose from $236 to $350/month — [Kilo](https://usekilo.com/gym-owner-makes-900k-yr-from-semi-private-training/)
- Industry: 91% of Mindbody-platform brands offered virtual classes, and CycleBar and others rented out bikes. Studios moved outdoors because online offerings were "less profitable." Club Industry estimated about $29.8B in US club and studio losses. A PTDC survey found 21% of trainers earned more after moving online — [IDEA: 5 Ways Fitness Businesses Created Revenue in Pandemic Times](https://www.ideafit.com/5-ways-fitness-businesses-have-created-revenue-in-pandemic-times/); [CNBC, Jul 25, 2020](https://www.cnbc.com/2020/07/25/coronavirus-fitness-studios-gyms-move-classes-outdoors.html); [IDEA: Online training and the post-pandemic landscape](https://www.ideafit.com/online-training-and-the-post-pandemic-fitness-landscape/); [WellnessLiving: PT trends 2021](https://www.wellnessliving.com/blog/personal-trainer-trends-7-trends-that-will-define-2021-for-trainers/)

**Inflation / cost of living (2022–2026)**
- Average gym membership price rose about 14% from Jan 2019 to Aug 2022, less than the cost-of-living increase — [Wodify: gym membership prices since COVID-19](https://www.wodify.com/blog/behind-the-numbers-gym-membership-prices-on-the-rise-since-covid-19)
- Two-Brain dataset (vendor; clients mostly US but global): average 60-minute PT price was $72 (2020) and $71 (2021), flat to slipping. Average group membership was about $159–160/month (2021–2022) — [Two-Brain State of the Industry 2020 PDF](https://twobrainbusiness.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/12/2020-State-of-Industry-Final-Web.pdf); [Two-Brain: State of the Industry 2021](https://twobrainbusiness.com/two-brain-radio-state-of-the-industry-2021/); [Two-Brain: State of the Fitness Industry 2022](https://twobrainbusiness.com/state-of-the-fitness-industry-2022/)
- 2025 report medians: 1:1 PT studios $13,000 → $16,000/month, small-group PT gyms $11,000 → $16,667/month, big-group gyms $17,000 → $18,750/month. Two-Brain says the reasons for the 1:1 jump "aren't as clear" and suggests raising rates and moving toward semi-private — [Two-Brain: Critical Digits 2025](https://twobrainbusiness.com/critical-digits-2025/); [Two-Brain: 10 Reasons for Optimism in 2026](https://twobrainbusiness.com/2026-optimism/)
- Tres Kennedy (rural AL): $150 → $285/month in steps with no meaningful drop in signups (Case 5) — [Two-Brain: gym-doubles-price](https://twobrainbusiness.com/gym-doubles-price/)
- Fitness Together: same-studio revenue +5.1% YoY in the 2024 FDD, while unit count shrank 126 → 96 (2021–2023) — [FDD Exchange: Fitness Together 2024](https://fddexchange.com/fdd/fitness-together-2024/)
- Alloy grew from 13 units (2022) to 135+ (2025), yet average unit sales reportedly fell 4% from 2024 to 2025, possibly from the influx of new units — [Franchise Chatter 2025](https://www.franchisechatter.com/2025/05/07/alloy-personal-training-franchise-review-2025-costs-fees-news-average-revenues-and-or-profits/)
- Big-box contrast: Planet Fitness raised its Classic card from $10 to $15 in 2024, its first base-price increase in about 30 years, and analysts expected little pushback — [Athletech News](https://athletechnews.com/planet-fitness-price-hike-likely-to-benefit-gym-industry-analysts-say/)
- Two-Brain argues that gyms with more than 150 clients usually have the worst retention, and that 150 clients × $240 = $36,000/month ($432K/year) — [Two-Brain: Recession-Proof Revenue](https://twobrainbusiness.com/recession-proof-revenue/)

### Inferences
- Inflation-era evidence favours **raising prices on a higher-value offer** (semi-private, prescriptive coaching, minimum terms) over discounting. Cases with stepwise increases report little churn. This rests on a handful of vendor-reported cases.
- Hybrid and online coaching acted as a bridge in 2020–2021, not as a durable primary revenue line for studios. In-person small-group was the post-COVID growth vehicle (BOLT, Alloy).

### Gaps
- No independent US survey linking studio price increases to retention outcomes in 2022–2026. A widely circulated "41% raised prices / 27% retention drop" statistic came from an unverifiable source and was excluded.
- No documented US studio case of corporate wellness contracts or nutrition add-ons with revenue figures. The nutrition example (Transform Scottsdale) has no numbers.
- Hybrid-gym "20–25% revenue advantage" (attributed to IHRSA by MMCG) could not be verified — [MMCG](https://www.mmcginvest.com/post/the-u-s-fitness-industry-membership-revenue-and-trends-in-the-post-pandemic-era)
