## Q1. Concrete cases: business, service structure, pricing, operating policy, results and why it worked

### Takeaway
Japan has the best-documented cases. RIZAP Group shows both ends of the market: a high-ticket "results commitment" programme with a 30-day full refund, and chocoZAP, a ¥2,980 (¥3,278 incl. tax) unstaffed convenience gym that reached about 1.35M members before falling back. Below them, smaller 回数券 (session pack) and pay-per-visit chains such as Katagiri-juku and monthly low-price chains such as Apple GYM expanded quickly. The UK's best-sourced case is Ultimate Performance, a premium results-led PT chain that grew turnover and profit during the cost-of-living crisis. Australia's documented story is mostly a contrast: F45 franchise distress versus BFT's small-group strength model. I found no Australian case with independently audited results for a single small studio.

### Case J1 – RIZAP (Japan): results-commitment personal training with a full refund guarantee
- **Service structure:** A fixed-length body-make course of 16 or 24 sessions (often marketed as 2 months, 16 sessions) with 1:1 training and diet coaching. — [Saison Card RIZAP benefit page](https://www.saisoncard.co.jp/csmembers/diamond/wellness/rizap/)
- **Pricing:** Enrolment fee ¥55,000. The card-holder offer waives it on the 16/24-session courses and adds ¥50,000 cash back. — [Saison Card](https://www.saisoncard.co.jp/csmembers/diamond/wellness/rizap/). One summary article lists an enrolment fee of ¥50,000 and **¥348,000 total for 16 sessions**. That article is undated, so current pricing is **[VERIFY]**. — [travelbook.co.jp listing (search extract)](https://www.travelbook.co.jp/t-1026/topic-76564/)
- **Refund / commitment policy:** In 2015 RIZAP announced that the guarantee was "fully renewed": **any reason, full refund of the course fee within 30 days** (「30日以内であれば、いかなる理由でもコース料金全額を返金する内容に全面リニューアル」). — [digitalpr.jp press release (2015)](https://digitalpr.jp/r/12120) / [value-press](https://www.value-press.com/pressrelease/143517). Current card-partner terms say customers who use the 30-day full refund guarantee are excluded from the cash-back offer, which shows the guarantee still exists in 2020s materials. — [Saison Card](https://www.saisoncard.co.jp/csmembers/diamond/wellness/rizap/)
- **Measurable results (segment):** RIZAP関連事業 (the segment holding RIZAP personal training **and chocoZAP**) had **FY2025/3 revenue of ¥49.7bn (+20.5% YoY) and operating profit of ¥0.67bn, against a ¥4.1bn loss the year before**. — [RIZAP IR, Wealth Advisor report June 2025 (search extract)](https://www.rizapgroup.com/ir/investigation-reports/wealthadvisor/wealthadvisor202506.pdf) **[VERIFY]**
- **Gap:** I could not find a stand-alone member count or store count for the original RIZAP personal-gym brand in this session (see Gaps).

### Case J2 – chocoZAP (RIZAP Group, Japan): low-price, unstaffed "convenience-store gym"
- **Launch and structure:** Brand rollout began July 2022. It is described as a "convenience-store-style" gym: short workouts, unstaffed and app-run, with add-ons such as self-whitening, massage and laundry or karaoke in some sites. — [Japan Times, 20 Oct 2024](https://japantimes.co.jp/business/2024/10/20/gyms-karaoke-laundry); [ir.sse.or.jp RIZAP report](https://ir.sse.or.jp/data/pdf/140120231114590083.pdf)

- **Member and store trajectory (all from IR-derived sources; dates differ, so compare carefully):**
  - 14 Nov 2023: **1.01M members, 1,160 stores** in 40 prefectures, about 17 months after launch — [SSE/RIZAP IR report](https://ir.sse.or.jp/data/pdf/140120231114590083.pdf)
  - 14 Feb 2024: 1.12M members; more than 1,370 stores at 29 Feb 2024 — [search extract of RIZAP-sponsored article (president.jp/gendai)](https://president.jp/articles/-/75416) **[VERIFY]**
  - 14 Feb 2025: **1.337M members (+213k YoY), 1,787 stores** — [RIZAP FY25/3 Q3 supplementary material](https://ssl4.eir-parts.net/doc/2928/ir_material_for_fiscal_ym/173281/00.pdf)
  - 31 Mar 2025: 1,791 stores; 15 May 2025: **1.35M members (peak reported), 1,799 stores** — [RIZAP IR management page](https://www.rizapgroup.com/ir/management); [FISCO report Jun 2025](https://www.fisco.co.jp/wordpress/wp-content/uploads/FISCO/rizap20250619.pdf)
  - 13 Nov 2025: **1.105M members, 1,828 stores**. The drop is attributed mainly to free shareholder-benefit members leaving; paid members were described as roughly flat. — [Business Insider Japan, Nov 2025](https://www.businessinsider.jp/article/2511-rizap-interim-results/)

- **Operating-policy shift:** After rapid growth hit "growing pains" (complaints about cleanliness and machine faults; Toyo Keizai describes about a year of "雌伏", lying low), RIZAP paused aggressive openings, fixed operations, then **resumed openings with a franchise model**: 300 new domestic stores planned (200 directly operated and 100 franchised), plus transfers of directly operated stores to FC owners (32 FC stores reported). — [Toyo Keizai](https://toyokeizai.net/articles/-/918528?display=b); [Business Insider Japan (FC pivot)](https://www.businessinsider.jp/article/chocozap-s-slowdown/); [Business Insider Japan (300 stores)](https://www.businessinsider.jp/article/2511-rizap-interim-results/); [PR Times FC progress](https://prtimes.jp/main/html/rd/p/000000048.000121693.html). The RIZAP IR December 2025 report gives a domestic potential of 8,000 stores — [Wealth Advisor Dec 2025](https://www.rizapgroup.com/ir/investigation-reports/wealthadvisor/wealthadvisor202512.pdf)
- **Group results (IR):**
  - FY2025/3 consolidated (IFRS): revenue **¥171,090m (+5.2%)**; operating profit **¥1,882m against a ¥648m loss the year before**. This was the first operating profit in three years, but far below the original ¥6.3bn guidance. — [RIZAP IR management page](https://www.rizapgroup.com/ir/management); [kabutan/FISCO memo](https://kabutan.jp/news/marketnews/?b=n202506190893)
  - FY2026/3 guidance: operating profit **¥11.0bn**. H1 FY2026/3: revenue ¥80.67bn (–3.5% YoY), operating profit ¥2.985bn. — [Business Insider Japan Nov 2025 (search extract)](https://www.businessinsider.jp/article/2511-rizap-interim-results/); Toyo Keizai headline "営業利益は19億→110億へ" — [Toyo Keizai](https://toyokeizai.net/articles/-/936103?display=b)
  - **Conflict:** One analyst report gives "¥172.0bn revenue (+0.5%), ¥11.0bn OP". This looks like the FY2026/3 forecast, not FY2025/3 actuals. — [SSE report 2025-06-27](https://www.sse.or.jp/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/2928-20250627.pdf) **[VERIFY]**
- **Why it worked / limits:** A price below a typical PT session per month, no staff, and short visits made it an "affordable entry tier" while real wages fell in Japan from 2022. Heavy upfront investment (direct openings plus ad spend) caused losses until FY2025/3. Profit came back by lifting profitability per store rather than by member growth: members fell from 1.35M to 1.105M while operating profit guidance rose. This is my inference from the cited figures.

- **Why it worked (inference):** This is a body-transformation, results-led PT product, the closest UK analogue to RIZAP. The affluent customers it targets were less affected by the squeeze than budget-gym customers. **Gap:** I did not find a sourced price list or retention policy in this session.

## Q2. Which formats grew, what happened to prices and retention, and which policies protected revenue

### Takeaway
Across all three markets, the formats that gained were (a) cheap, low-commitment entry tiers (chocoZAP; Japanese monthly and pay-per-visit personal gyms; UK budget gyms), (b) small-group or semi-private PT that cuts the price per person by half or more while raising trainer revenue per hour, and (c) premium results-led PT for less price-sensitive customers (Ultimate Performance, RIZAP). Multi-year, high-fixed-cost franchise group studios (F45) suffered. Revenue was protected by refund guarantees that lower the perceived risk of a big purchase, enrolment-fee waivers, session packs and pay-per-visit options in place of long contracts, and franchising to move capex onto owners. I found little hard, sourced data on freeze/pause or referral policies.

### Cited findings
- **Small-group PT economics (UK):** £15–35 per person per session versus £35–80 for 1:1. A trainer charging £50 for 1:1 earns £50/hr, while five clients at £20 earn £100/hr. — [Institute of Personal Trainers](https://instituteofpersonaltrainers.com/blog/small-group-personal-training); [Mark Ludlow PT guide (£18–30 small group vs £40–70 1:1)](https://markludlowpersonaltrainer.com/personal-trainer-prices-uk-hertfordshire-guide/); [Form Fitness Manchester (£20–40 vs £50–80)](https://weareformfitness.com/training/cost-guide-manchester/). These are trade and vendor sources, not independent research.
- **Small-group PT economics (Australia):** Semi-private costs A$30–70 per person and small-group A$20–50 per person, against A$70–150 for a 1:1 session. — [Wild Physio Fitness](https://wildphysiofitness.au/blogs/physiotherapy/how-much-does-a-personal-trainer-cost-in-australia); [Fitness Image](https://fitnessimage.com.au/how-much-does-a-personal-trainer-cost-in-australia/); [goingrate.com.au](https://goingrate.com.au/wellness/gym-membership-cost) (vendor guides)
- **UK price-sensitive cancellations:** Nearly 30% of consumers who cancelled a gym membership cited affordability as the main reason. Private club visiting has risen since 2024. — [Mintel UK health & fitness clubs report (2025)](https://store.mintel.com/report/uk-health-and-fitness-clubs-market-report). Interviewees in a PA cost-of-living piece compared a £30 monthly fee to a weekly food shop and moved to free online workouts. — [PA training feature](https://pa-training.shorthandstories.com/cost-of-living-how-have-people-adapted-their-exercise-habits-because-of-the-crisis/index.html) (undated)
- **UK low-cost price levels:** The average low-cost gym fee is now above £25/month. — [Ashbourne summary of LeisureDB 2025](https://ashbourne-memberships.com/blog/2025/08/06/state-of-the-uk-fitness-industry-report-2025-gym-owner-highlights/). Mintel reported a "boom time for budget exercise". — [Mintel press](https://www.mintel.com/press-centre/boom-time-for-budget-exercise/)
- **Risk-reversal policy:** RIZAP's 30-day full refund for any reason. — [digitalpr.jp](https://digitalpr.jp/r/12120)
- **Fee waivers instead of price cuts:** RIZAP waived its ¥55,000 enrolment fee for partner card holders. Katagiri-juku ran ¥30,000-off enrolment campaigns. — [Saison Card](https://www.saisoncard.co.jp/csmembers/diamond/wellness/rizap/); [minhyo.jp](https://minhyo.jp/katagirijuku)
- **Shift away from lump-sum courses in Japan:** Industry rankings describe growth in "継続型" (ongoing) personal gyms, both unlimited monthly and pay-per-visit/session pack, for mid- to long-term clients. Examples are First Class Trainers (56 stores by Jan 2025), Katagiri-juku and Apple GYM. Meanwhile the short-course player 24/7Workout saw revenue fall 21.3%. — [note 継続型(通い放題) ranking](https://note.com/hidesharez/n/n4b149510cda0); [TRAINERS GUILD 2025](https://note.com/guild_2025/n/nca3da1b46866)
- **Community and retention (UK):** The LeisureDB 2025 report highlights community as increasingly important to growth, retention and satisfaction for independent operators. — [Ashbourne summary](https://ashbourne-memberships.com/blog/2025/08/06/state-of-the-uk-fitness-industry-report-2025-gym-owner-highlights/)
- **Australia, boutique economics:** Boutique studios spread fixed costs over fewer members, so instructor quality, class utilisation and lease discipline decide success. Market growth slowed to 2.8% in 2024 as cost of living hit discretionary spend. — [Ken Research AU gym market report](https://www.kenresearch.com/industry-reports/australia-gym-and-fitness-centre-market) (commercial report summary; moderate reliability)

### Inferences
- The "barbell" pattern holds in all three countries. Very cheap, low-commitment access (chocoZAP, UK budget gyms) and high-value outcome-based PT (RIZAP, UP) both held up. The middle, meaning undifferentiated group studios with high fixed costs (F45), was squeezed.
- For a small PT studio, the replicable levers are: (1) move clients from 1:1 to semi-private or small-group at roughly 40–60% of the 1:1 price while raising trainer revenue per hour; (2) sell tiered weekly or monthly packages (AU example) or session packs (Japan) rather than one large upfront fee; (3) use risk reversal (refund or results guarantee) and fee waivers rather than cutting the headline price; (4) add structured fixed-length outcome programmes (12-week, 2-month/16-session) as the acquisition product.
- chocoZAP shows that member counts can be inflated by free or promotional members (shareholder perks). Paid-member and per-store profit metrics are the better health signals.

### Gaps
- No sourced data on freeze/pause policies, referral programmes or churn rates for small studios in any of the three countries.
- No independently verified UK or Australian single-studio case with revenue before and after a format change. The searches found only vendor and pricing guides, not news interviews.

## Q3. RIZAP model and chocoZAP member counts and profitability (IR)

### Takeaway
RIZAP's core offer pairs a premium fixed course (16 or 24 sessions, about ¥348k per 16-session course in one undated source, plus a ¥50–55k enrolment fee) with a 30-day, any-reason full refund. Its 2022 pivot to chocoZAP (¥2,980/¥3,278 per month) grew to a reported peak of about 1.35M members (May 2025). After heavy losses, the group returned to an operating profit of ¥1.88bn in FY2025/3. Members then fell to about 1.1M by Nov 2025, partly because free shareholder-perk members left, and the company switched to franchise-led openings with an ¥11bn operating profit target for FY2026/3.

### Cited findings
- 30-day any-reason full refund (2015 renewal) — [digitalpr.jp](https://digitalpr.jp/r/12120)
- 16/24-session courses; ¥55,000 enrolment fee; refund users excluded from cash-back — [Saison Card](https://www.saisoncard.co.jp/csmembers/diamond/wellness/rizap/)
- FY2025/3: revenue ¥171,090m (+5.2%); operating profit ¥1,882m (prior: –¥648m) — [RIZAP IR](https://www.rizapgroup.com/ir/management)
- RIZAP関連事業 FY2025/3: revenue ¥49.7bn (+20.5%); operating profit ¥0.67bn (prior –¥4.1bn) — [Wealth Advisor Jun 2025](https://www.rizapgroup.com/ir/investigation-reports/wealthadvisor/wealthadvisor202506.pdf) **[VERIFY]**
- chocoZAP members: 1.01M (Nov 2023) → 1.337M (Feb 2025) → 1.35M (May 2025) → 1.105M (Nov 2025) → 1.161M (May 2026); stores 1,160 → 1,787 → 1,799 → 1,828 → 2,000+ (Jul 2026) — sources as in Case J2 above
- FC pivot plus 300-store plan (200 direct, 100 FC); 8,000-store domestic potential — [Business Insider Japan](https://www.businessinsider.jp/article/2511-rizap-interim-results/); [Wealth Advisor Dec 2025](https://www.rizapgroup.com/ir/investigation-reports/wealthadvisor/wealthadvisor202512.pdf)
- FY2026/3 operating profit guidance ¥11.0bn; H1 OP ¥2.985bn on revenue of ¥80.67bn (–3.5%) — [Business Insider Japan](https://www.businessinsider.jp/article/2511-rizap-interim-results/) (search extract) **[VERIFY]**

### Inferences
- The refund guarantee lowers the perceived risk of a ¥300k+ discretionary purchase, which matters when household budgets are tight. It also commits the trainer side to delivering outcomes. Refund-rate data would be needed to measure its cost.
- chocoZAP turned RIZAP's brand into a mass, low-ticket subscription. Profitability came only after the company stopped chasing member counts and fixed store operations.

### Gaps
- Original RIZAP personal-gym member and store counts, refund usage rates, and FY2026/3 actual results (due around May 2026) were not retrievable in this session because the IR PDFs could not be fetched.

## Q4. Market data (UK / Japan / Australia)

### Takeaway
UK fitness reached record highs by March 2025 (LeisureDB: 11.3M members, £6.5bn). The Australian personal-trainer market shrank (IBISWorld: A$433.1m in 2024, –4.1%). For Japan, I could not get the Yano Research personal-training market size or the latest METI fitness-club series in this session.

### Cited findings
- **UK (LeisureDB 2025, 12 months to 31 Mar 2025):** 7,202 gyms (+2.8%, close to the 2019 peak of 7,239); **11.3M members (+5.4%)**; 16.6% penetration; **market value £6.5bn (+9.3%)**. Public-sector gym numbers fell slightly while public membership and value hit all-time highs. — [PAF Media](https://www.paf-media.co.uk/leisure-db-releases-the-state-of-the-uk-fitness-industry-report-2025); [Health Club Management](https://www.healthclubmanagement.co.uk/health-club-management-news/Industry-metrics-are-at-an-all-time-high-says-LeisureDB-David-Minton/356016); [free report (Flippingbook)](https://online.flippingbook.com/view/408285620/38-39/)
- **UK consumer:** Affordability was cited by nearly 30% of membership cancellers — [Mintel 2025](https://store.mintel.com/report/uk-health-and-fitness-clubs-market-report). Time and money remain barriers to membership — [Leisure Opportunities / YouGov](https://leisureopportunities.co.uk/news/Time-and-money-still-barriers-to-gym-membership-according-to-YouGov/353499)
- **Australia:** Personal trainers market **A$433.1m in 2024, –4.1% in 2024; –5.5% CAGR 2018–2023** — [IBISWorld AU personal trainers](https://img.ibisworld.com/australia/market-size/personal-trainers/4195). Gym market growth slowed to 2.8% in 2024 because of cost-of-living pressure — [Ken Research](https://www.kenresearch.com/industry-reports/australia-gym-and-fitness-centre-market)
- **Japan:** The METI 特定サービス産業動態統計 includes フィットネスクラブ monthly (e.g. [April 2024 release](https://www.meti.go.jp/statistics/tyo/tokusabido/result/pdf/hv202404sj.pdf)), but search extracts did not show the figures. An older METI analysis notes that フィットネスクラブ user numbers are cumulative visits (延べ利用者数), not members — [METI analysis](https://www.meti.go.jp/statistics/toppage/report/bunseki/pdf/h26/h4a1502j1.pdf). The personal-gym store count is estimated at about 5,000–5,500 (2024; blog estimate) — [THE FITNESS](https://thefitness-personal.jp/personal-gym-market-data/)

### Inferences
- The UK industry as a whole grew in money terms through the cost-of-living crisis, with low-cost fees rising to above £25. A shrinking share of wallet was therefore not universal; operators with value or affordable positioning gained.
- The Australian decline in the personal-trainer category, together with F45 distress, suggests 1:1 PT spend was cut. That points to semi-private and small-group formats as the logical defensive product there, though I found no direct data confirming the shift.
